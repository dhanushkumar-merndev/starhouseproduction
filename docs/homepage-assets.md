# Homepage artwork and editing

The homepage uses the existing procedural Three.js camera model and original Star logo. The intro starts on solid charcoal and reveals the white hero background (`--paper: #ffffff`) during the camera pullback; the rig itself remains opaque. Scroll moves the camera from right to left, enters its monitor for the services gallery, then pulls back to separate the lens, body and battery. The dismantled rig leaves the frame before the mountain footer reveals a grounded photographic crew scene and gently drifting fog. The hero has no landscape, paper-grain overlay or decorative colour gradients. Lenis smooths wheel and anchor scrolling, while touch uses native inertia. Reduced-motion preference disables smooth scrolling and the pinned journey, presenting services and footer in normal page flow.

## Editing

- Hero copy and the composition around the rig: `src/components/camera/camera-scene.tsx`. Besides the headline it carries three reel cards, the chapter index down the left margin, the counts beside the social row, the social row and the scroll cue, with a backdrop layer holding the ghosted `FILM` word, the arc, the crosshair and the film strip in the corner. Every layer is positioned to stay clear of the 3D rig, which sits wider on screen than in a flat mock-up, so the desktop placements are tuned in a `min-width: 1025px` block and the decorative layers drop away below 1024px, the reels and social row below 620px.
- The index rail sits in the left margin, outside the copy column and vertically centred, and counts the four beats of the scroll story: 01 the hero, 02 the "Find the feeling" copy, 03 the projected services screen, 04 the exploded rig — the footer holds 04 rather than dropping the rail. The scene reports those beats as `chapter` in `SceneState` (the hero's two halves are told apart there, not flattened into one "hero"), and `railPosition` in `camera-scene.tsx` maps them onto the four numbers. Its tick sits outboard of the number, toward the page edge — inboard it reached into the copy column at every width below 1920. It sits at `z-index: 6`, above the projected monitor (4) and the parts overlay (5), either of which would otherwise hide it for a whole chapter. It dips out and back on each change, and stays hidden until the rig is revealed, because the dip writes an inline opacity that would otherwise hold it lit over the intro; the counts beside the social row run up to the figure already in the markup, so they still read correctly if the timeline never plays.
- The header only inverts once the projected monitor screen genuinely sits behind it. That is measured from the portal's own rectangle rather than taken from the chapter name, because the chapter starts well before the screen has grown to fill the frame. Two things the measurement has to account for: past the services chapter the screen is faded to `opacity: 0` while still filling the rect, so opacity is tested alongside visibility; and the rig keeps easing for about half a second after the last scroll event, so the measurement carries on for a second after each one rather than deciding the tone mid-move.
- Reel thumbnails are the four panels of `public/images/visual-directions.webp`, positioned the same way the projects grid uses them, so the hero needs no new artwork. The headline lands on a GSAP timeline — each line rises out of its own `.hero-line` mask, with the eyebrow, standfirst and call to action following behind it. The start pose is set in JS rather than CSS, so if the timeline never runs the hero is simply visible instead of stranded off-screen. Two things about that timeline are easy to get wrong:

  - Its GSAP context is scoped to the whole studio, not to `.hero-copy`. The reels, counts, social row and scroll cue all sit outside `.hero-copy`, so a context scoped there silently matched nothing and none of them animated.
  - It runs once per page load, like the intro. Coming back to the homepage from the nav re-mounts the component, and replaying the landing there reads as a glitch rather than an entrance.

- The counts run up from zero on the same timeline. They are zeroed as the hero lands rather than when the tween starts, otherwise the finished figure shows for the length of the delay and then snaps back to count up.
- The film strip is driven by `--journey`, the scene's own scroll progress, rather than a second scroll listener: the frames run through the gate as the page moves, and the strip drifts with them. The drift lives in the `min-width: 1025px` rule because the desktop override there replaces `transform` outright.
- One shell measurement, `--gutter` in `globals.css`, positions the header, the hero copy and every section, so the navigation and the body share a left and right edge at any width.
- Camera intro and scroll framing: `src/components/camera/studio-scene.ts`; the shot windows are in `scroll-story.ts`. The projected screen reaches full-bleed at `SERVICES_BROWSE.start`, and that climb is kept short on purpose — a long one leaves the page half-grown, with a white header over what is already mostly a dark screen. `SERVICES_BROWSE` is shared with the keyboard-focus handler, which has to map a browse offset back onto exactly that window.
- The projects page is now the reel wall alone; the old filterable grid (`projects-section.tsx`) was removed and lives in commit 9e48b5d if it is ever wanted back.
- The projects page opens with the reel wall (`src/components/site/reel-wall.tsx`): a curved wall of cards on a CSS 3D arc, turned by drag, horizontal wheel, arrow keys or the pager. The arc bows *away* from the viewer: the middle card sits furthest back and the cards toward the edges swing forward, so perspective alone grows them and they run off both sides of the frame. Bowing it the other way — middle nearest, neighbours receding — produces a fanned stack of shrinking cards, which is a different thing entirely and was the first attempt. Only the card facing you carries its caption; the rest are images on a wall. Cards name their own image, size and position, so the four frames of the contact sheet and the two standalone photographs can sit side by side. Pressing a card off the centre turns the wall first and opens second, so the detail always grows out of a card that is facing you; the panel is animated from the pressed card's rectangle with GSAP, and closes on Escape, the backdrop or the button, restoring focus to the card. It is deliberately not the React Bits `CircularGallery`: that component is WebGL (ogl), which would need raycasting to make a card pressable and a second WebGL context alongside the Three.js hero.
- `/contact` is its own route with its own stylesheet, `contact-page.module.css`, rather than more classes in the shared `studio-sections.module.css`. The addresses and phone number in `contact-intro.tsx` are placeholders in one list, so swapping in the real ones is a single edit.
- Scroll chapter timings: `src/components/camera/scroll-story.ts`; monitor corner projection: `src/components/camera/screen-portal.ts`.
- The same live services board stays aligned to the physical monitor throughout entry and exit. Edit its cards in `src/components/site/monitor-services.tsx`. Its responsive masonry layout is adapted from React Bits, with the source link and full license alongside `src/components/site/reactbits-masonry.tsx`.
- Exploded-view service labels: `camera-scene.tsx`. The footer and its links: `src/components/site/mountain-footer.tsx` and its CSS module. The footer uses one continuous photographic scene: crew, distant mountains and solid foreground ground. A slow pan moves the whole image together; two faint fog layers drift across the distant mountain region above the feet. Separate desktop and portrait images preserve the composition. Reduced-motion users get still imagery.
- Navigation and glass styling: `src/components/site/site-header.tsx` and its CSS module.
- Separate pages: `/services`, `/projects`, and `/about-us`, under `src/app/(studio)/`.
- Page content: `services-section.tsx`, `projects-section.tsx`, and `about-section.tsx` in `src/components/site/`, with shared `studio-sections.module.css`.
- `/services` presents the five services on an option wheel (`services-wheel.tsx`), which drives the detail panel beside it. The wheel itself is vendored in `src/components/ui/option-wheel/` from React Bits (MIT + Commons Clause, see the LICENSE.md kept alongside it); only its CSS import and two React-hooks lint patterns were adapted. Wheel/touchpad scrolling over it rotates the wheel rather than scrolling the page, which is the component's own behaviour; dragging is disabled below 700px so a swipe scrolls the page.
- The homepage keeps the camera story, monitor gallery, capability labels and mountain footer. The project enquiry is at `/services#enquiry`; its local draft form is in `contact-section.tsx`. Separate pages use `site-footer.tsx`.
- Smooth scroll and image parallax: `src/components/site/smooth-scroll.tsx`. Wheel input is read as a direction only, never a distance: while the wheel is turning the page travels at a fixed `WHEEL_SPEED`, so a hard flick and a gentle nudge move at the same rate. The position is advanced once per animation frame and handed to Lenis as a target, so Lenis still does the easing. `WHEEL_SPEED` at the top of that file is the single dial for pace.
- `/services` renders on black: `(studio)/layout.tsx` marks dark routes and `.studioShell[data-theme="dark"]` redefines `--paper`/`--ink` for everything inside, header included. The header carries both marks and shows the right one: `public/logo.png` on light routes, `public/logo-dark.png` (white strokes, red keeper) wherever `data-theme="dark"` or the header's own `data-tone="dark"` applies, which also covers the monitor chapter on the homepage.
- Page transitions: `src/components/site/page-transition.tsx`, a lens iris that opens on the arriving page. A route change commits before the component can react, so the aperture opens rather than closing over the page being left. It never plays on first load, only on navigation: the guard compares the current route against the one the component mounted on, because effects are invoked twice in development and a simple first-run flag fired the iris on load.
- The camera intro runs undisturbed: scrolling during it neither moves the scene underneath it nor cuts it short, and the page is held at the top until it finishes (`overflow: hidden` for the user, plus the `scroll-lock` flag the page scroller checks so its own programmatic scrolling cannot get past). A 9s failsafe releases the hold if the scene never reaches its resting state. `scrollbar-gutter: stable` keeps the hold from shifting the layout.
- The camera intro plays once per page load. `camera-scene.tsx` keeps an `introPlayed` flag at module scope, which survives client-side navigation back to the homepage and resets on a hard refresh; on a return visit the scene is skipped straight to its resting state. There is no skip button.
- `/services` takes over the wheel: while the option block sits across the middle of the viewport, scrolling turns the wheel one service at a time, and once the options run out in that direction the event is left alone and the page scrolls on. The wheel component's own wheel handling is switched off there (`wheelInput={false}`) so it cannot swallow the hand-off.
- Login is a simple button, as requested. The brief form only prepares a local draft for copying; it does not send data.
- Visual-direction cards are concept artwork, not claims of completed client projects. Replace these with actual work when available.

## Generated assets

Mode: built-in image generation, then WebP encoding at quality 85 for local delivery. Originals remain in the image-generation output directory. No external image requests are made by the homepage.

## Typography

Inter is served locally from `public/fonts/inter-latin-variable.woff2` (latin subset, variable weight 400–900); the accompanying SIL Open Font License is `public/fonts/OFL-Inter.txt`. Source: Google Fonts' `ofl/inter` collection. It is the single family for the site — body copy, navigation and display headings — and matches the grotesque bold of the Star wordmark in the logo lockup. `--font-brand` in `globals.css` carries the stack; `--font-mono` stays Courier New for eyebrows and labels. Caveat (`public/fonts/caveat-latin-variable.woff2`, licence in `OFL-Caveat.txt`, Google Fonts' `ofl/caveat`) is `--font-hand`. It is currently unused — the hero's handwritten "Create / Capture / Inspire" callout was removed — and the face is kept only so the token has something to resolve to.

- Desktop background: `public/images/footer-grounded-crew.webp` (1672 × 941; about 172 kB).
- Portrait background: `public/images/footer-grounded-crew-mobile.webp` (941 × 1672; about 166 kB).
- Fog overlay: `public/images/footer-fog.webp` (1600 × 533, alpha preserved; about 177 kB).
